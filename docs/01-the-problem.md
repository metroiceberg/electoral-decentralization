# The Problem

## A systems-design question

The project begins with a distinction:

> **The ability to win an election is not necessarily the same thing as the ability to govern effectively.**

Campaigning requires valuable capabilities such as communication, organization, coalition building, fundraising, and public appeal. Those capabilities do not necessarily measure every capability required for governing.

The question is whether elections can provide voters with additional evidence about governing capability without removing their final authority.

## Concentration of intermediary power

Political parties can provide coordination, policy development, voter education, organization, and legislative cooperation. At the same time, any organization that becomes a necessary gateway to representation can accumulate influence over who reaches voters.

The design question is:

> **How can intermediary institutions remain useful without becoming necessary gatekeepers of political representation?**

## Questions to investigate

- How should governing capability be evaluated?
- Who should perform qualification?
- How can qualification avoid ideological gatekeeping?
- How can citizens challenge qualification decisions?
- How can political machines be prevented from replacing party machines?
- How should representatives be selected at each level?
- How should endorsements be presented?
- What voting method best preserves meaningful citizen choice?
- How can decentralization coexist with effective national coordination?
- What safeguards prevent capture by local elites?

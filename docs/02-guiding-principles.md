# Guiding Principles

These principles are the current foundation of the project. They are intentionally broader than any particular electoral mechanism.

## 1. Popular sovereignty

The authority to choose representatives ultimately belongs to the people.

## 2. Decentralization of electoral power

Electoral influence should not be unnecessarily concentrated in a small number of political organizations, institutions, intermediaries, or individuals.

## 3. Governing capability matters

The ability to govern should be treated as distinct from popularity. The working hypothesis is that higher office should draw from people who demonstrate exceptional knowledge, reasoning, judgment, integrity, competence, adaptability, and leadership.

The precise definition and measurement of these qualities remain open research questions.

## 4. Qualification is not ideological selection

A qualification process should evaluate governing capability rather than political ideology.

## 5. Citizens retain the final choice

Qualification must not become a mechanism by which an elite class selects rulers for the public.

> **Qualification asks whether a person is capable of governing. Election asks whether citizens want that person to govern.**

## 6. Endorsements are information, not ownership

Political parties and other organizations may endorse candidates. An endorsement should not make an organization the owner or gatekeeper of a candidate.

## 7. Mechanisms remain provisional

If evidence demonstrates that a mechanism increases concentration of power, creates unacceptable capture risks, or fails its intended purpose, it should be reconsidered.

## 8. Transparency

Important qualification criteria, procedures, evidence, decisions, and institutional rules should be publicly inspectable to the greatest practical extent.

## 9. Accountability

Every layer exercising delegated authority should remain accountable to the people below it.

## 10. Decentralization without paralysis

The objective is not fragmentation for its own sake. Electoral power should be distributed while government retains sufficient capacity to coordinate and act.

## 11. Evidence over attachment

Changing a mechanism in response to strong evidence is progress, not failure.

## 12. Open scrutiny

The project should be challengeable, forkable, and improvable by people who do not agree with its current design.

# Executive Summary

## The question

Representative government must solve two distinct problems:

1. identifying people capable of governing; and
2. determining whom citizens want to represent them.

This project explores whether separating those functions could improve representation while preserving popular sovereignty.

## Initial direction

The current concept uses multiple levels of representation. Representatives at one level may participate in identifying or qualifying candidates for the next level, while the broader electorate retains authority to choose among qualified candidates.

The model therefore separates:

- **Qualification** — evidence of governing capability.
- **Endorsement** — public signals from political parties, civic organizations, unions, advocacy groups, or others.
- **Election** — the citizens' final choice.

## Central objective

The ultimate objective is **decentralization of electoral power**.

The project asks whether institutional design can distribute candidate development, evaluation, information, and selection more broadly without preventing effective government.

> **The principles are the foundation. The mechanisms are hypotheses.**

# The Proposed Model

**Status: Initial hypothesis**

The current model separates three functions.

## 1. Qualification

A representative body at one level may identify candidates for the next level who demonstrate exceptional governing capability.

Potential evidence includes knowledge, reasoning, judgment, integrity, competence, systems thinking, leadership, adaptability, institutional understanding, and demonstrated performance.

No final qualification framework has been established.

## 2. Public endorsement

Qualified candidates remain free to articulate their own political philosophy.

Political parties, unions, civic organizations, advocacy groups, professional organizations, and others may endorse candidates.

Multiple organizations may endorse the same candidate.

**Endorsement is information—not ownership.**

## 3. Election

Citizens choose among qualified candidates.

The electorate retains the final authority.

## Conceptual flow

```
Citizens

   v
Candidate Qualification

Qualified Candidates

   +---- Political / civic endorsements

```

The exact hierarchy remains an open design question.

## Unresolved questions

- What qualifies as evidence of governing capability?
- Who qualifies candidates?
- How are qualifying representatives selected?
- How are conflicts of interest handled?
- How are qualification decisions appealed?
- How can political-machine capture be prevented?
- Can citizens place an additional candidate before the electorate?
- How should independent candidates participate?
- What voting method should be used?
- What constitutional changes would be required?
